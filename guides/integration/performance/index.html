INTEGRATION/PERFORMANCE

Performance

Crushform’s cost comes from a few knobs: the atlas and its slots, the resolution of each lattice, how often the plugin rebuilds collision and dirt, and how many tyres, debris pieces and sounds may run at once. This page names each knob with its default. It gives no frame times, because they depend on your project. Measure your own project with the commands in the last section of this page.

The atlas and its slots

Section titled “The atlas and its slots”

Every damaged body takes one slot in the atlas, and the slot holds its lattice. The atlas is the render target in UCFSettings::Atlas. Its surface is capped at 4096 texels on each side (MaxAtlasSize in CFTypes.h), and the number of slots is the surface divided by one slot, UCFSettings::SlotWidth by UCFSettings::SlotHeight.

  • A lattice fits a slot when its ResX times ResZ is at most SlotWidth, and its ResY is at most SlotHeight. The default profile’s UCFProfile::Resolution is 16 by 8 by 8, which fits the default slot of 256 by 8.
  • A slot must hold the smallest lattice, 4 by 4 nodes. A slot smaller than 16 texels wide or 4 texels high leaves the dents invisible, and the log says so.
  • The atlas must be linear B8G8R8A8: an RTF_RGBA8 render target, or a BGRA8 texture without sRGB. Any other format leaves the dents invisible too.

When every slot is taken, a new damaged body takes the slot of the farthest damaged body, but only if that body is farther from the camera than the new one. The farthest body then waits and draws undamaged until a slot is free again. A body at the same distance as the farthest one waits too.

The lattice and the profile

Section titled “The lattice and the profile”

The lattice is the cost that a profile sets for each body.

  • UCFProfile::Resolution (16 by 8 by 8 by default) is the node count of the lattice. A bigger lattice needs a bigger slot, as above.
  • UCFProfile::bDents (on): when it is off, hits and crush presses never dent or scratch the body. Dirt still works.
  • UCFProfile::DirtScale (1): every dirt deposit on the body is multiplied by it. At 0 the body gets no dirt grid at all.
  • UCFProfile::MaxPlausibleDeltaV (40 m/s): a harder hit is reported at this limit, and the server rejects anything above it.

Reports and collision

Section titled “Reports and collision”
  • UCFSettings::MaxReportsPerSecond (60) is how many reports per second the server accepts from one component. A sending client’s bucket holds that many reports; the server’s bucket is 1.5 times deeper. UCFSettings::MaxReportsPerBatch (8) is the most reports a driving client sends in one frame.
  • UCFSettings::ScrapeReportInterval (0.1 s) is the time between two scrape reports of one component.
  • UCFSettings::CollisionRebuildThreshold (2 cm) and UCFSettings::CollisionRebuildInterval (0.5 s) control when a dented body’s collision is built again. A rebuild happens only when a node has moved this far, and no sooner than the interval.
  • UCFSettings::CollisionBlockTolerance (1.5 cm) and UCFSettings::MaxCollisionBlocks (96) control how many blocks a dented body’s boxes and convexes are cut into. MaxCollisionBlocks is the most blocks that all the boxes and convexes of one body are cut into together. A value at or below the number of those shapes cuts nothing: each stays one hull.

Dirt

Section titled “Dirt”
  • UCFSettings::DirtUpdateInterval (0.2 s) is the time between two dirt updates of one body.
  • UCFSettings::DirtReferenceSpeed (8 m/s) is the tyre speed at which a surface’s rates apply as written. UCFSettings::MaxDirtSpeedScale (2) caps how much faster tyres multiply them.
  • UCFSettings::DirtEventInterval (0.5 s) and UCFSettings::DirtEventThreshold (0.02) limit the dirt-changed events of one body.

Tyres

Section titled “Tyres”

The tyre budget decides which wheels deform. UCFTireSettings::Scope picks the mode, and the budget’s values are project settings under Crushform Tire; see Project settings. Each value is also a console variable, which overrides the project value when a console command or a command-line argument sets it. All of them are described in the console reference:

  • Crushform.Tire.MaxWheels (30): the tyres that may deform at once. The viewer’s own vehicle always deforms, even past the cap.
  • Crushform.Tire.Radius (4000 cm): the distance from the camera within which other vehicles’ tyres may deform.
  • Crushform.Tire.LodDistance1 (1500 cm) and Crushform.Tire.LodDistance2 (3000 cm): the distances where a tyre traces a thinner ray fan, and then the thinnest fan against simple collision.
  • Crushform.Tire.UpdateInterval (0.25 s): the time between two budget passes.
  • Crushform.Tire.Hysteresis (0.15): how far a granted tyre’s boundaries widen, so that a tyre on a boundary does not flicker.
  • Crushform.Tire.OnScreenTolerance (0.5 s): how long after it was last drawn a wheel still ranks as on screen.
  • Crushform.Tire.Enable (1): set to 0 and every tyre relaxes back to round.

The sleep of resting wheels has no console variable: UCFTireSettings::SleepDistance (1 cm) and UCFTireSettings::SleepAngleDeg (0.5 degrees): a settled wheel that stays within these can fall asleep, and it stops tracing until its pose changes. A negative SleepDistance turns sleep off.

Breakables and debris

Section titled “Breakables and debris”
  • UCFBreakableSettings::MaxLiveDebrisPieces (96) is the live debris pieces per world. The oldest is recycled beyond it.
  • UCFBreakableSettings::MaxPersistentDebrisPieces (64) counts apart the pieces of definitions whose debris lasts until respawn.
  • UCFBreakableSettings::DebrisMaxSpawnDistanceCm (10000 cm) is the distance from the local camera beyond which a break spawns no debris.
  • UCFBreakableSettings::AnyBodyCheckIntervalSeconds (0.1 s) is how often a fence with units that break from any body looks for bodies near them.

Impact feedback and rolling FX

Section titled “Impact feedback and rolling FX”
  • UCFImpactFeedbackSettings::MaxAudibleDistanceCm (6000 cm) and UCFImpactFeedbackSettings::MaxEffectDistanceCm (8000 cm): impacts farther than these from the local camera play no sound and spawn no effect.
  • UCFImpactFeedbackSettings::MaxVoiceSeconds (3 s) is the longest a played sound holds a voice slot. Each row of an impact set has its own FCFImpactFeedbackEntry::MaxConcurrentVoices (4 by default), the number of its sounds that may play at once.
  • UCFWheelSettings::FXTuning stops the rolling FX of a vehicle while it is slow. FCFRollingFXTuning::SleepSpeedKPH is 1 km/h by default.

Check that it works

Section titled “Check that it works”

Press Play In Editor on a level with a damaged body, then open the console.

  • Crushform.Stats prints the atlas first, in this form. A growing number of waiting bodies or evictions means the atlas is full.

Then it prints one line for each damageable actor in the world: server lattice where this machine holds the damage, client view where it draws the server’s. The line goes on with the actor’s generation, its pending reports and chunks, and its counters: hits, reports sent, accepted and rejected, collision builds with the longest one in milliseconds, and the part hulls. A dedicated server process, which draws nothing, prints Crushform atlas: none in this process (a dedicated server draws nothing). instead of the first line.

  • stat Crushform puts the core module’s timed steps on screen, such as the deform tick, the dirt update and the body collision rebuild, each in milliseconds. The other stat groups are CrushformTire, CrushformBreakable, CFBreakableInstances, CrushformDebris, CFPropContact, CFImpactFeedback, CrushformVehicle, CrushformChaos, CrushformWheel, CrushformEngine, CrushformPlayground and CrushformDebug.

Troubleshooting

Section titled “Troubleshooting”

Dents stop on some damaged vehicles. Cause: the atlas is full. The farthest damaged bodies wait for a slot and draw undamaged until one is free. Crushform.Stats shows the waiting count and the evictions. Fix: make UCFSettings::Atlas larger. The slot count is the atlas size divided by the slot size, up to the 4096-texel cap, so a larger atlas holds more damaged bodies at once.

Dents never appear, and the log says the slot is too small. Cause: UCFSettings::SlotWidth is below 16 texels or UCFSettings::SlotHeight is below 4. Fix: raise the slot size to at least 16 by 4 texels. The default is 256 by 8.

Dents never appear, and the log names the atlas’s pixel format. Cause: the atlas is not linear B8G8R8A8. An sRGB texture, or any other format, is refused. Fix: use an RTF_RGBA8 render target, or a BGRA8 texture with sRGB turned off.
